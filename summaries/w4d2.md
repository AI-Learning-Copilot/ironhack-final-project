## Overview

This lesson covers the fundamentals of Natural Language Processing (NLP), including text preprocessing techniques, feature extraction methods, and practical applications such as news clustering using unsupervised learning.

## Key Concepts

- **Natural Language Processing (NLP)**: A field of AI focused on enabling computers to understand and interact with human language.
- **Text Preprocessing**: The process of cleaning and preparing text data for analysis.
- **Feature Extraction**: Techniques to convert text into numerical representations for machine learning.
- **Common NLP Tasks**: Include text classification, sentiment analysis, named entity recognition, and more.
- **Unsupervised Learning**: Techniques like clustering that do not require labeled data.

## Detailed Notes

### Natural Language Processing (NLP)

### Text Preprocessing

1. **Tokenization**: 
   - Splitting text into individual words or tokens.
   - Example: "The quick brown fox" → ["The", "quick", "brown", "fox"].

2. **Removing Punctuation**: 
   - Cleaning text by eliminating punctuation marks to focus on words.

3. **Stopwords**: 
   - Common words (e.g., "the", "is") that are often removed as they do not add significant meaning.

4. **Stemming and Lemmatization**:
   - **Stemming**: Reduces words to their base form (e.g., "running" → "run").
   - **Lemmatization**: Returns the valid base form of a word (e.g., "better" → "good").

### Feature Extraction Techniques

1. **Bag of Words (BoW)**:
   - Represents text as a vector of word counts, ignoring grammar and word order.
   - Variants include Binary BoW (presence/absence) and Count BoW (word counts).

2. **TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency)**:
   - A measure that evaluates the importance of a word in a document relative to a corpus.
   - **Components**:
     - **TF**: Frequency of a term in a document.
     - **IDF**: Importance of a term across documents.
   - Formula: 
     \[
     idf_i = 1 + \log\left(\frac{1 + \text{total documents}}{1 + \text{documents with term } i}\right)
     \]

3. **N-grams**:
   - Sequences of 'n' items (words or characters) from a text.
   - Examples: Unigrams (1 word), Bigrams (2 words), Trigrams (3 words).

### Implementation Details

- **Document-Term Matrix**: A matrix representation where rows are documents and columns are words.
- **Pairwise Distance Matrix**: Measures similarity between documents using metrics like Euclidean distance.

## Examples & Applications

- **News Clustering**:
  - A practical application of NLP where news articles are clustered based on content.
  - Steps include tokenization, lemmatization, stopword removal, and vectorization using BoW or TF-IDF.
  - KMeans clustering is applied to group articles into coherent topics.

- **Naive Bayes Classifier**:
  - A probabilistic classifier used for text classification tasks.
  - Trained on labeled data to classify new text based on learned patterns.

## Key Takeaways

- NLP is essential for enabling machines to understand human language.
- Text preprocessing is crucial for preparing data for analysis.
- Feature extraction techniques like BoW and TF-IDF are foundational for text representation.
- Unsupervised learning methods, such as clustering, can reveal patterns in unlabelled data.
- Practical applications of NLP include sentiment analysis, text classification, and clustering.
